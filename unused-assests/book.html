Chapter I

"The further the spiritual evolution of mankind advances, the more certain it seems to me that the path to genuine religiosity does not lie through the fear of life, and the fear of death, and blind faith, but through striving after rational knowledge."

— Albert Einstein

The auditorium was large, the audience scattered. He had spoken for twenty minutes and his voice had not once risen above its normal conversational level. His words seemed almost casual, yet everyone in the room had listened.

Gaal Dornick had come to Trantor with a feeling of awe and a feeling of trepidation. He had never seen the Imperial planet before.

5

Chapter I Continued

The foundation of all understanding is knowledge itself. As Gaal looked upon the infinite structures of Trantor, he realized that his journey had only just begun. The great mathematician Seldon had foreseen this moment, and now the weight of destiny rested upon his shoulders.

Years of study had prepared him for this, yet nothing could truly prepare one for the reality of standing before greatness itself. The universe was vast, and his role in it seemed impossibly small, yet somehow significant.

Chapter II

"Science may be described as the art of systematic over-simplification—the art of discerning what we may safely ignore."

— Karl Popper

The view from the upper levels of the city was frightening in its immensity. Trantor was a world of metal and stone, the entire surface of the planet a single city. Every square foot of ground had been used, and none was wasted.

Gaal found himself overwhelmed by the sight. Nowhere on his home world was there anything to compare to this.

7

Chapter II Continued

The complexity of the city was almost incomprehensible. Billions upon billions of humans lived and worked within these structures, each one a small part of an incomprehensibly large whole. Yet Seldon's science of psychohistory had shown that this vast humanity could be understood through mathematics.

It was this revelation that had drawn Gaal to Trantor. He wanted to understand, to learn from the master himself, to become part of something greater than he could ever achieve alone.

8

Chapter III

"The cumulative effect of small things over long periods is enormous."

— Darwin

Hari Seldon was a legend even before his death. The greatest mathematician of his time, the founder of psychohistory—the science that could predict the future of masses of human beings through mathematics. It seemed impossible.

Yet the evidence was overwhelming. The Foundation had been created according to Seldon's predictions. The very nature of their mission on Terminus had been outlined four hundred years before their arrival.

9

Epilogue

The power of prediction was the power to shape destiny. Seldon had understood this fundamental truth and had used it to create the Foundation. Through centuries of careful planning and mathematical precision, he had charted a course that would guide humanity through its darkest hours.

Gaal now understood his purpose. He would be part of this grand design, a small thread in the vast tapestry of human civilization. And in that understanding came both humility and determination.

10

The Foundation stood as a beacon of hope in the gathering darkness of the crumbling Galactic Empire. Though countless challenges lay ahead, the vision of Hari Seldon would guide them forward, through the centuries to come.
